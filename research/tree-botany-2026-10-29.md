# Botanical references for Mneme's eight voxel trees

Researched 29 October 2026. Scope: botanical reference and rendering recommendations only; no application changes. Sources are university horticulture/extension, the RHS, Kew, and government forestry publications. Linked galleries are reference material, not downloaded assets or permission to redistribute photographs.

## How to use this note

**Botanical evidence** below paraphrases cited sources. **Rendering proposal** is an art-direction inference, not a measured botanical specification. No source establishes a universal branch angle in degrees: use the stated upward/spreading/drooping relationships and vary them. Leaf arrangement describes placement on shoots; it does not mean every major limb forms an exact paired fork.

Keep the existing eight user-facing labels. Use species exemplars to make morphology coherent, without implying that every oak, birch or maple has this shape. Select healthy, open-grown, established trees for the canonical silhouette. Do not combine a sapling trunk, veteran crown and dwarf cultivar foliage into one supposed mature specimen.

## 1. Oak — English oak, *Quercus robur*

**Botanical evidence.** A broad rounded crown can be as wide as the tree is tall; the trunk is short and stout, with increasingly fissured dark gray-brown bark. Leaves are alternate, simple and rounded-lobed; twigs carry clustered terminal buds. These support an irregular branching impression rather than maple-like paired leaf placement. [NC State: English oak](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/quercus-robur/). OSU also describes the massive short trunk and provides winter twig and habit references. [OSU: English oak](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/quercus-robur).

**Rendering proposal.** Choose a healthy mature park tree: broad shoulders, substantial base flare, several thick ascending-to-spreading scaffold limbs, then asymmetric smaller forks. Preserve branch-to-crown connections and gaps between lobed canopy masses. Make it the heaviest trunk among the broadleaf set. Small blunt leaf groups can imply rounded lobes without individual leaf geometry.

**Pitfalls.** A thin pole under a perfect ball loses the oak; repeated identical radial branches look mechanical. A narrow column would suggest a selected upright cultivar rather than this exemplar; OSU explicitly separates those forms. [OSU](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/quercus-robur).

**Photographic pages.** OSU gallery: plant habit, trunk/bark, winter twigs, leaves. [Kew oak profile](https://www.kew.org/plants/oak-tree) additionally includes trunk, leaf and reproductive details.

## 2. Birch — silver birch, *Betula pendula*

**Botanical evidence.** The youthful pyramid becomes rounded/oval with age. Fine young twigs hang downward; the bark is whitish, with substantially darker old trunk bark. Leaves are alternate, simple, pointed and doubly toothed, often diamond-shaped; autumn foliage is yellow. OSU cautions that this species does not peel like paper birch. [OSU: silver birch](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/betula-pendula). NC State also identifies brown warty young twigs and white larger branches. [NC State: silver birch](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/betula-pendula/).

**Rendering proposal.** Choose an established single-stem tree, taller and lighter than the oak, with ascending scaffolds and downward fine tips. Keep the upper trunk pale, the mature base darker and rougher, and leaf masses small enough to reveal twigs and sky. A modest base flare should meet the terrain; multiple stems are optional specimen variation, not the definition of birch.

**Pitfalls.** Uniform white bark down to the soil, giant peeling curls, oak-sized limbs, or a solid willow curtain. Let only the finer outer growth droop strongly.

**Photographic pages.** OSU gallery has habitat, summer/winter habit, leafy shoots, bark and older-tree bark; NC State includes form, bark and leaf details. Distinguish species photographs from the explicitly named dwarf/weeping cultivars.

## 3. Maple — upright Japanese maple, *Acer palmatum*

**Botanical evidence.** This is a small, variable tree, often multi-trunked. Spreading branches and a low rounded crown give a layered appearance. Leaves are opposite, simple and deeply palmately lobed; young stems are smooth and green to reddish brown, older branches grayer. Summer foliage can be green; color and weeping/upright habit depend heavily on cultivar. [NC State: Japanese maple](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/acer-palmatum/). OSU independently documents the multi-trunk habit, slender twigs and green foliage with autumn scarlet/orange/yellow. [OSU: Japanese maple](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/acer-palmatum).

**Rendering proposal.** Use an upright, green-leaved species-type tree, with a low fork, slender smooth trunks, and shallow spreading layers separated by air. Suggest opposite leaf placement at small shoots, while varying the surviving larger forks. Reserve strong red/orange foliage for autumn. This provides a smaller, flatter and more delicate silhouette than oak without making it a bonsai.

**Pitfalls.** Permanent crimson implies a deliberately chosen red-leaved cultivar. A low waterfall mound instead implies a dissected/weeping selection. These are real alternatives, but not this canonical form. [RHS Japanese-maple guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/acer/japanese-maples/growing-guide).

**Photographic pages.** OSU: spring/summer/autumn habit and winter twigs. NC State: many named cultivar photographs plus a botanical-detail illustration; do not mix them indiscriminately.

## 4. Cherry Blossom — *Prunus* 'Kanzan' (also *P. serrulata* 'Kanzan', historically 'Kwanzan')

**Exemplar correction.** Use 'Kanzan' for the requested pink art direction. RHS accepts *Prunus* 'Kanzan' and lists *P. serrulata* 'Kanzan' as a synonym. It starts as a stiff vase and spreads with age; crimson buds open into vivid purplish-pink double flowers, accompanied by coppery young foliage. [RHS: 'Kanzan'](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/99553/prunus-kanzan-d/details).

**Botanical evidence.** OSU describes stiff ascending, spreading branches and deep-pink double flowers in hanging groups; leaves emerge bronze and may become orange-bronze in autumn. [OSU: 'Kanzan'](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/prunus-serrulata-kanzan). The Japanese flowering-cherry group has alternate simple serrated leaves and horizontal bark lenticels. [OSU: Japanese flowering cherry](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/prunus-serrulata).

**Rendering proposal.** Choose an established upright-spreading vase: a visible trunk divides into ascending arms before spreading outward. Put clustered blossom masses on connected branch tips and short growth, with a few bronze new-leaf accents; switch to green leaf masses after flowering. Keep its center more open and its arms steeper than maple.

**Pitfalls.** A permanently pink summer crown, weeping architecture, or one opaque pink sphere. Yoshino (*P. × yedoensis*) is a valid different exemplar, but its pink buds become almost white flowers before leaves; do not turn Yoshino hot pink. [OSU: Yoshino](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/prunus-yedoensis).

**Photographic page.** OSU 'Kanzan': flowering habit, leaf/flower emergence, flower clusters, old trunk/bark, autumn habit and winter buds.

## 5. Weeping Willow — weeping *Salix babylonica*, with hybrid caution

**Botanical evidence.** The stout, furrowed gray-black trunk supports a broad rounded crown and thin pendant branches toward the ground. Narrow, finely serrated leaves occur alternately along shoots; reddish/yellowish-brown twigs carry small catkins rather than conspicuous petal flowers. Cultivated weeping willows are often hybrids with *S. alba* or *S. euxina*; OSU explicitly says its photographed willows are not authenticated as pure *S. babylonica*. [OSU: weeping willow](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/salix-babylonica). NC State provides species descriptions of bark, slender leaves and spring catkins. [NC State: weeping willow](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/salix-babylonica/).

**Rendering proposal.** Retain *S. babylonica* as the conceptual exemplar, but treat unverified photographs as weeping-willow morphology references. Use stout rising and arching structural arms, then multiple long thin falling shoots; leaves follow those shoots as broken curtains. Preserve an interior trunk glimpsed through irregular openings. The canopy should widen near the top and trail toward the ground, with a grounded, flared base.

**Pitfalls.** Making every structural branch point straight down from the trunk, disconnected hanging foliage, a sealed green tent, or confusing ordinary pendant growth with a corkscrew cultivar. Do not claim an exact hybrid identity from appearance.

**Photographic pages.** OSU: spring/summer habit and pendant branches/catkins. NC State includes three photographs plus descriptive bark and foliage fields; its gallery captions do not identify each organ. Cultivar identity still requires care.

## 6. Pine — mature Scots pine, *Pinus sylvestris*

**Botanical evidence.** Pyramidal youth develops into a wide, irregular, rounded or flat-topped crown; spreading lower branches die off. Orange-brown flaky upper bark is distinctive. Needles are stiff, twisted, blue-green and bundled in pairs. Geographic and cultivated forms vary considerably. [OSU: Scots pine](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/pinus-sylvestris). Government forestry also distinguishes orange-red upper bark from fissured lower bark and confirms the loss of lower branches with age. [Forestry and Land Scotland: Scots pine](https://forestryandland.gov.scot/living-and-working/learning-and-education/trees/scots-pine).

**Rendering proposal.** Deliberately choose an established open-grown specimen with a relatively clear lower bole, orange upper branching and uneven spreading limbs. Put broken needle tufts along the living outer shoots, with two-needle bundles suggested only at close scale. Make a sparse irregular upper crown, rather than another full cone. Ground the trunk with a modest flare; large exposed roots are optional local variation.

**Pitfalls.** A dense Christmas-tree triangle duplicates spruce; broadleaf balls hide the needle architecture. A wholly orange base discards the important bark transition. Avoid copying dwarf, columnar, creeping or weeping selections into the species default.

**Photographic page.** OSU gallery: several habits, stand versus isolated trees, trunk and large-branch bark, needle branches, cones and winter habit.

## 7. Jacaranda — *Jacaranda mimosifolia*

**Botanical evidence.** Juveniles can have a long unbranched trunk; established trees have a vase-like crown that can become irregular. Bark is rough dark gray with fine vertical fissures. Opposite bipinnate leaves divide twice into small leaflets; blue-purple funnel flowers form panicles and can cover a temporarily leafless crown before new foliage. [NC State: jacaranda](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/jacaranda-mimosifolia/). RHS describes a rounded mature tree, fernlike leaves, and spring/early-summer flowers, and identifies the species as frost tender. [RHS: jacaranda](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/9429/jacaranda-mimosifolia/details).

**Rendering proposal.** Use a mature warm-climate tree with a longer visible trunk than maple, rising forks and broad outward crown lobes. Fine, airy green sprays should imply compound leaves; violet blossom groups should follow connected outer branching rather than replace the whole tree with a solid purple ball. Keep its branching more open and foliage finer than the cherry. A subdued base flare is sufficient.

**Pitfalls.** Purple leaves in every season; large maple-shaped leaves; a juvenile stick bearing a huge mature flower crown. Seed-grown trees may take years to flower, so immediate seedling blossoms are a deliberate garden fantasy. [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/jacaranda-mimosifolia/).

**Photographic pages.** NC State: flowering street-tree habit, flower carpet, leafy flower-bearing branch and bipinnate leaf; bark and fruit are described in text, not identified in its photo captions. RHS includes a flowering reference. Its frost-tender cultivation guide explains that UK outdoor/indoor specimens may never reproduce the warm-climate flower display. [RHS growing guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/jacaranda/growing-guide).

## 8. Spruce — Norway spruce, *Picea abies*

**Botanical evidence.** A straight regular trunk and conical crown carry whorled branches: upper ones rise, lower ones droop. [European Commission JRC: Norway spruce atlas chapter](https://forest.jrc.ec.europa.eu/media/atlas/Picea_abies.pdf). Secondary branchlets droop; short four-sided green needles surround the shoot rather than forming pine bundles. Mature cones hang down. Old trees can become open. [OSU: Norway spruce](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/picea-abies). Bark becomes scaly; NC State also documents the needle attachment pegs. [NC State: Norway spruce](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/picea-abies/).

**Rendering proposal.** Choose a healthy established species tree with a dominant narrow leader and a continuous tapering outline; retain visible gaps and uneven branch ends. Build tiered main branches around the trunk, with smaller pendant branchlets; use narrow green sprays close to the branch skeleton. Preserve this hierarchy even at low voxel count. The trunk/base should be mostly concealed by low branches, rather than becoming a broadleaf pedestal.

**Pitfalls.** Perfect stacked solid cones, identical rotational symmetry, pine-like leaf tufts, or erect mature cones. Avoid making the whole tree a willow: the leader and main framework remain legible while secondary growth hangs.

**Photographic pages.** OSU: full habit, branches, branchlets/needles, trunk/bark and cones. JRC chapter: isolated mature tree and bark/resin detail. Prefer species photographs over dwarf or extreme weeping cultivars.

## Canonical developmental forms

These are **proposed visual stages**, not a conversion from Mneme's clock into exact biological years. The cited species accounts above support the developmental changes, but do not supply one universal age at which each form appears.

| Label | Young form | Canonical established form | Variation to keep controlled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oak | Slender stem, smaller ascending framework | Short heavy trunk; broad uneven crown | Forest-grown taller boles and veteran dead tops are different stories |
| Birch | Narrow/pyramidal, delicate stem | Taller open oval crown; pendant fine tips; darkening base | Grafted weeping and dwarf forms are not the default |
| Maple | Slender upright or low-forking stem | Small spreading layers with smooth slender framework | Upright green-leaved form; no automatic weeping cultivar |
| Cherry Blossom | Stiff upright vase | Ascending arms spreading with age | 'Kanzan', not Yoshino, for strongly pink double blossoms |
| Weeping Willow | Rising framework with short hanging tips | Broad arching frame; long broken curtains | Hybrid identities and branchlet color vary |
| Pine | More pyramidal with lower branches | Clearer lower trunk; sparse irregular upper crown | Do not freeze mature pine in Christmas-tree youth |
| Jacaranda | Long juvenile stem, large compound leaves | Open broad vase/rounded crown | Warm-climate flowering stage; not cold-climate pot culture |
| Spruce | Narrow leader, stiffer young branches | Conical framework with pendant secondary growth | Old trees can open; avoid dwarf selected shapes |

For grounding, modest flares in these proposals are visual continuity choices, not species-diagnostic root measurements. Avoid inventing dramatic buttresses or identical exposed root stars on every tree. Mature height differences should inform relative proportions, not literal meter-scale trees on a miniature island.

## Phenology and four coarse season presets

The following is a **proposed artistic mapping** of the source-supported deciduous/evergreen and flowering behavior. It represents characteristic moments within a season, not a claim that flowers last all spring or leaf fall happens on a single calendar boundary. Geography, hemisphere, cultivar, tree age and weather shift actual dates. Do not infer biological changes from today's forecast temperature.

| Label | Spring preset | Summer preset | Autumn preset | Winter preset | Botanical basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oak | Fresh green leaf flush; optional subtle catkins | Full dark-green crown | Yellow/brown leaves with thinning | Mostly bare scaffold | [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/quercus-robur/), [OSU](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/quercus-robur) |
| Birch | Light green leaves; optional slender catkins | Airy green crown | Yellow leaves with thinning | Fine bare twig silhouette and pale trunk | [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/betula-pendula/) |
| Maple | Green new foliage on selected species-type form | Green layered crown | Red/orange/yellow leaf display, then thinning | Bare fine branches | [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/acer-palmatum/), [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/acer/japanese-maples/growing-guide) |
| Cherry Blossom | 'Kanzan' pink double blossom clusters and some bronze new leaves | Green foliage; no mass blossoms | Orange/bronze foliage with thinning | Bare ascending framework | [OSU 'Kanzan'](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/prunus-serrulata-kanzan), [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/99553/prunus-kanzan-d/details) |
| Weeping Willow | Early narrow leaf flush; optional tiny catkins | Full green hanging shoots | Yellowing, relatively late leaf loss | Bare pendant shoots | [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/salix-babylonica/) |
| Pine | Retained blue-green needles; modest new-shoot accents | Blue-green needle tufts | Retained needles | Retained needles | [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/pinus-sylvestris/) |
| Jacaranda | Blue-purple bloom on a sparse/temporarily leafless crown, then green flush | Fine green crown; optional fading late flowers | Green or gently thinning warm-climate crown | Thinner/bare dry-season illustration; no implied frost survival | [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/jacaranda-mimosifolia/), [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/9429/jacaranda-mimosifolia/details) |
| Spruce | Retained green needles with lighter new growth as a rendering accent | Green branch sprays | Retained needles | Retained needles | [OSU](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/picea-abies), [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/picea-abies/) |

**Jacaranda qualification.** Native seasonally dry forests lose foliage in the dry season, while flowering timing and leaf retention differ across cultivated climates. A temperate four-season control cannot reproduce that globally. Label the mapping as a warm-climate illustrative cycle; a tropical/neutral app season should default to a healthy green, non-dormant crown rather than force an imagined snowy winter. If the product needs a flowering tropical/neutral view, make that an explicit artistic choice, not a location-derived botanical prediction. The frost-tender species is usually grown under protection in the UK and rarely flowers there. [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/jacaranda-mimosifolia/), [RHS growing guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/jacaranda/growing-guide).

**Conifer qualification.** Evergreen means retaining an evergreen crown, not that each individual needle persists forever. The coarse model can keep pine and spruce canopies throughout the four seasons without simulating needle turnover. Seasonal lighting/snow cover should not turn evergreen needles into deciduous autumn foliage.

## Evidence limits and implementation handoff

- Preserve silhouette and scaffold topology first, species bark and leaf/flower organization second, fine serrations and individual petals last. This priority is a rendering recommendation.
- Shape contrast should survive grayscale: massive oak; light upright birch; low layered maple; ascending cherry vase; willow curtains; irregular high pine crown; open fern-textured jacaranda; tapering spruce leader and pendant secondary sprays.
- Linked galleries contain multiple ages, cultivars, pruning histories and licenses. Source descriptions and gallery captions were checked; this note does not authenticate every photographed specimen or extract numerical geometry from pixels. OSU explicitly warns about its willow identification.
- No photograph was downloaded or reused, and no image generation was performed. No application tests/build were run because only this research note was added.
